Orbital Blues: Wanderer

A solo RPG about outlaws facing their final days in the frontier galaxy

Wanderer Quickstart Guide

Your solo journey into the Frontier Galaxy starts here.

What is Wanderer?

These are your final days as an outlaw. Your crew: long gone. Your ship: sold for the scraps that held it together. This is a game about reflection — about your final showdown with the Troubles you've been running from.

Wanderer is a solo mode of Orbital Blues that requires no Storyteller. You're playing your character's final story: reliving memories from their outlaw life and building a playlist as you face the Troubles that have haunted them. You can journal detailed responses or keep rough notes—the depth is entirely up to you.

What You Need

  • 4 six-sided dice (d6)
  • A deck of playing cards (standard 52-card deck, plus jokers optional)
  • Poker chips or small tokens (for tracking Blues Tokens)
  • Paper or a journal (for documenting your story)
  • A way to create a playlist (music streamer, CD burner, cassette, or notes on which songs fit)
  • A copy of Orbital Blues (physical or digital—you'll reference character creation and base rules)
  • An Orbital Blues character sheet

The Four Phases of Wanderer

Your Wanderer game has four distinct phases. Each one involves drawing cards and responding to prompts about memories. The game builds toward a final confrontation.

Phase 1: Hearts

What you loved and lost

You draw cards from the Hearts suit. Each card presents a memory prompt related to things you cared about, relationships, or moments that mattered.

For each card:

  1. Draw from the deck
  2. Roll a Grit Check (roll 3d6, choose two dice, add your Grit stat)
  3. On a total of 8 or higher, you succeed and keep the card
  4. On a total of 7 or lower, you fail—discard the card to the Troubles Deck
  5. Respond to the card's prompt with a success or failure narrative
  6. Each successful Blues Check earns you 1 Blues Token—keep track of these

Phase 2: Diamonds & Clubs

What you chased and what you survived

Drawing continues through the Diamond suit (cards of fortune and pursuit) and Club suit (situations you barely made it out of).

Same mechanics as Phase 1, but different Stats:

  • Hearts & Spades: Use Grit Checks
  • Diamonds: Use Savvy Checks (for clever plays and fortune)
  • Clubs: Use Grit Checks (for survival and endurance)

Phase 3: What's Come Home to Roost

Facing the Troubles you've accumulated

This is where the game shifts. You've been drawing and discarding cards throughout Phases 1 and 2. Now you use those discarded cards—your failures, your regrets—to create a final challenge.

Setup:

  1. Lay all cards from your Troubles Deck face up
  2. From these cards, select the 5 that make the best possible poker hand
  3. Set that hand aside as a Trouble Hand
  4. Repeat: create as many complete poker hands as possible from the remaining cards
  5. Lay all Trouble Hands in front of you, visible
  6. Return excess cards to the main deck and shuffle

Phase 4: Where That Leaves You

Your final reckoning

After defeating (or being defeated by) all Trouble Hands, you draw one final card. This card's prompt determines your ending.

Making Checks & Rolling

Checks Against The Odds vs. With The Upper Hand

When rolling a Check:

  • Against The Odds: Roll 3d6, then discard the highest die. Add your chosen Stat to the two lowest dice.
  • With The Upper Hand: Roll 3d6, then discard the lowest die. Add your chosen Stat to the two highest dice.

The default is against the odds—that's the frontier life. You can choose to roll with the upper hand if narrative circumstances justify it (you prepared well, fortune favors you, etc.).

Difficulty Threshold

  • 8 or higher: Success. Keep the card, gain 1 Blues Token, respond to the success prompt
  • 7 or lower: Failure. Discard to Troubles Deck, respond to the failure prompt (no Blues Token)

Turns & Drawing Cards

When you take a Turn, draw a card from the top of the deck, roll a Check corresponding to its suit, keep or discard the card depending on the result, then consult the appropriate prompt for that card.

Each Turn:

  1. Draw the top card
  2. Check the suit (Hearts, Diamonds, Clubs, Spades)
  3. Roll the appropriate Check (see stat associations above)
  4. Keep card on 8+, discard on 7 or lower
  5. Read the matching prompt (success or failure)
  6. Answer the prompt, journaling or noting as much detail as you wish

Prompts ask you to recall situations—memories that give you the blues. They could be as short as a combat turn or as long as a full arc from a standard campaign. You decide the depth. When unsure where to turn, lean on your Trouble.

Blues Tokens & Your Heart

Blues Tokens: Earned when you succeed on a Blues Check. These represent moments of clarity and small victories.

Heart: Your character's resilience (emotional and physical). You start at 12. During Phase 3 when you Attack your Troubles, the remaining die in your roll reduces your Heart. If your Heart drops to 0 or below, you're defeated—but the story isn't necessarily over; it's just reached its conclusion.

Building Your Playlist

As you play, you're building a soundtrack to your character's life. This is an optional but thematic element.

  • Each Spade card you draw adds a song to your playlist
  • Song suggestions are built into prompts (e.g., "A song that comes easy," "A song that should be covered by androids," "A song that justifies")
  • Keep a list or create an actual playlist
  • The songs you choose should reflect the emotional truth of your character's final days

Attacking & Defeating Troubles

In Phase 3, when you confront a Trouble Hand:

  1. Roll an Attack: 3d6, choose two dice, add your appropriate Stat
  2. On 8+: You hit. The target (the Trouble Hand) loses one card from its hand, or you overcome that aspect
  3. Damage to You: The remaining die value reduces your Heart
  4. The prompt tells you the narrative outcome of your success or failure

You can keep attacking a Trouble Hand until all its cards are gone, or you can move to the next Trouble. It's your story.

Example Turn Walkthrough

You draw the 5 of Hearts from the deck.

Hearts use Grit Checks, so you're confronting a memory about something you cared about.

The prompt reads:

A bullet that you put yourself in the path of. Success: Who did you take the bullet for? Failure: Where does a fragment still sit within you?

You roll 3d6: you get a 2, 4, and 5.

Your Grit stat is 2. You choose the two highest dice: 4 and 5. Total: 4 + 5 + 2 (Grit) = 11. That's a success!

You gain 1 Blues Token. Now you answer the success prompt: Who did you take the bullet for?

You journal: "It was Marcus. He was running point into the Vault, and a private security guard had line of sight. I tackled him aside—the round went clean through my shoulder. Marcus never knew how close it was."

Keep the card (it's a win), mark your Blues Token, and move to your next Turn.

Quick Reference: Poker Hands (Best to Worst)

  1. Royal Flush — 10-A of same suit
  2. Straight Flush — Five-card run of same suit
  3. Four of a Kind — Four cards of same value
  4. Full House — Three of a kind + pair
  5. Flush — Five cards of same suit
  6. Straight — Five-card run of different suits
  7. Three of a Kind — Three cards of same value
  8. Two Pair — Two sets of same-value cards
  9. Pair — Two cards of same value
  10. High Card — No other hand can be made

Character Creation Guide

Building your interstellar outlaw.

Step 1: Concept & Name

Before diving into mechanics, envision your character.

Think about:

  • Who were you before you became an outlaw?
  • Why did you turn to the frontier life?
  • What makes you distinctive?
  • What's your role in a crew or in your final days?

Step 2: The Three Core Stats

To reflect a character's abilities, Orbital Blues uses three core stats: Muscle, Grit, and Savvy.

Muscle

Muscle determines the strength of your arm and the bulk under your jacket. It is the force behind the switchblade, and it determines how much or how little blood comes out if you're on the wrong end. A character with high Muscle could be well-built, in peak physical fitness, or packing a cybernetic arm or two. A character with low Muscle might be frail, thinly-built, or out of shape.

Used for: Forcing open doors, hitting something/someone, heavy lifting, physical endurance

Grit

Grit is your ability to never give up, and never give in. It is the tenacity to chase the intergalactic dream and the will to watch one's friends come and go with the passing of the years. A character with high Grit could be a grizzled veteran, a methodical doctor, or a stone-cold broker. A character with low Grit could be cowardly, flippant, or indecisive.

Used for: Resisting pain, continuing on without rest, keeping a straight face, willpower

Savvy

Savvy is the speed of your arm, the pull of your trigger finger. It is the speed of the cat, and the wit of the joker. Savvy represents both physical and mental agility. Characters with high Savvy might be charismatic, graceful, or resourceful. A character with low Savvy might be clumsy, dim-witted, or obtuse.

Used for: Quick reflexes, lying convincingly, drawing weapons, charm and persuasion

Allocating Your Stats

Player characters tend to be very good at one thing, pretty good at another, and average at a third. Allocate your bonuses as follows:

  • +2 to one Stat (your best skill)
  • +1 to another Stat (pretty good at this)
  • +0 to the final Stat (average, but still better than normal people)

Step 3: Heart

Your character's resilience—both physical and emotional.

Heart = 8 + your Muscle stat

Examples:

  • Character with Muscle +2: 8 + 2 = 10 Heart
  • Character with Muscle +0: 8 + 0 = 8 Heart
  • Character with Muscle -1: 8 + (-1) = 7 Heart

Step 4: Trouble

Every outlaw has something chasing them.

Troubles are summations of a character's past misgivings, misdeeds, vices or regrets. They fuel your individual and group narratives.

Common Trouble Examples:

  • Addiction – To a substance, gambling, or risky behavior
  • Betrayal – You betrayed someone, or were betrayed
  • Debt – A significant financial or personal obligation
  • Revenge – Someone wants you dead, or you want them dead
  • Guilt – You carry responsibility for someone's death or ruin
  • Loss – Someone or something precious was taken from you
  • Authority – You're wanted by a powerful organization

Step 5: Blues

A measure of your experience and growth.

Starting Blues: You begin with 0 Blues and gain them through play, primarily when your Troubles affect the narrative. Blues track your character's accumulated weight and richer history.

Step 6: Gambits

Your special talents and reliable assets.

Gambits are talents, tricks and reliable assets that characters call upon to get jobs done. They reflect a character's past, ties or abilities.

Step 7: Equipment

Choose a few pieces of equipment that make sense for your character:

  • Weapons: Ranged weapons, melee weapons, or improvised options
  • Gear: Protective armor, tools, communication devices
  • Memento: One personally significant item from your past
  • Specialized equipment: Tech, medical kit, or something unique to your expertise

Name: Viktor Soren

Concept: Enforcer for a mega-corporation who went rogue after they ordered a hit on his sister.

Stats:

  • Muscle: +2
  • Grit: +1
  • Savvy: +0

Heart: 8 + 2 = 10

Trouble: Corporate Revenge—Syndicate Corp wants you dead.

Starting Gambit: Armored—Cybernetic enhancements and protective gear

Equipment: Combat rifle, heavy body armor, sister's watch (memento), lock-breaker kit

Tips for Great Character Creation

  1. Let your Trouble drive you. It should create interesting problems and narrative hooks.
  2. Don't optimize, characterize. A character with interesting weaknesses is more fun.
  3. Think about your character's voice. How do they speak? What's their personality?
  4. Picture yourself in the role. If you're not excited, revise.
  5. Embrace failure. Low stats aren't penalties—they're character development opportunities.
  6. Your Memento matters. Choose something that would hurt to lose.
